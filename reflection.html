Reflection

Professional reflection

This module gave me my first real experience of building something end-to-end and putting it out into the world for other people to see, rather than just submitting an assignment to be marked. Deciding on structure, writing content that represents me honestly, and thinking about how a stranger — like a potential employer — would read the site all pushed me to think differently about my work than I usually do for coursework.

Working on this portfolio also made me reflect on the range of skills I've picked up across my modules so far. Between the Java programming, database and ERD design, and networking theory, I've started to see how these areas connect rather than existing as separate subjects. Building the site itself was also a small technical exercise in its own right, since I coded it directly in HTML, CSS, and JavaScript rather than using a drag-and-drop builder.

01

My strongest IT professional skill

Programming logic — breaking a problem down into classes, structures, and steps, which came through clearly in the Hospital Patient Admission System project. I'm comfortable working through a problem methodically even when the language or tools are new to me.

02

The skill I'd most like to improve

Professional communication — explaining technical work clearly to people who aren't necessarily technical themselves. Writing the About Me and project descriptions for this portfolio actually highlighted this gap, since summarising a project in a few sentences for a general audience is a different skill from writing code comments or a technical report for a lecturer.

03

How I plan to develop that skill

By practising plain-language summaries of my projects as I build them, rather than only writing documentation aimed at other developers. I'd also like to get more comfortable presenting my work verbally, since that's another form of the same skill.

04

How this portfolio supports my future studies and career

As I take on more projects during my studies, I can keep adding to it, which means I'll arrive at internship or job applications with a live, evolving body of work rather than having to build something from scratch at the last minute. It also gives me a professional presence I can point people toward, which matters given my goal of eventually running my own business in cybersecurity or network engineering.
